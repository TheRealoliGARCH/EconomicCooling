# Dynalene HC grade screening at 20°C

## Scope

This report compares manufacturer-published thermophysical properties at 20°C for five exact Dynalene HC grades. HC-10/20/30/40 values are transcribed from the 2025 Dynalene HC Engineering Guide. HC-50 values are retained from the 2020 HC Technical Data Sheet in the current master dataset. The source revisions are therefore not harmonised.

This is a descriptive property screen, not a controlled heat-transfer experiment, product qualification, or thermohydraulic-equivalence finding.

## Published values at 20°C

| Grade | Specific heat (kJ/kg K) | Thermal conductivity (W/m K) | Density (kg/m³) | Dynamic viscosity (mPa s) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HC-10 | 3.320 | 0.524 | 1190 | 1.80 |
| HC-20 | 3.212 | 0.523 | 1237 | 2.10 |
| HC-30 | 3.077 | 0.519 | 1275 | 2.50 |
| HC-40 | 2.930 | 0.509 | 1315 | 2.70 |
| HC-50 | 2.701 | 0.505 | 1340 | 3.20 |

All five grades report the same broad directional pattern across the published values at this temperature: increasing grade number is associated with lower specific heat and thermal conductivity, and higher density and viscosity. This is a descriptive pattern in manufacturer tables, not a causal composition law. The guide does not disclose the grade suffix as a mass or volume percentage, so no numerical concentration should be inferred from the grade designation.

## Interpretation

At 20°C, HC-10 has the highest reported specific heat and the lowest reported density and viscosity in this five-grade set. HC-50 has the highest reported density and viscosity and the lowest reported specific heat and thermal conductivity. These differences can affect Reynolds number, pressure drop, heat capacity rate, and convective heat transfer in different directions.

A property-only ranking is insufficient. The net system result depends on the imposed constraint:
- equal mass flow;
- equal volumetric flow; or
- equal pump electrical power.

The ranking may change across these operating regimes and with temperature, geometry, pump curve, surface roughness, and heat-transfer correlation.

## Evidence limitations

1. HC-10/20/30/40 data are from the 2025 manufacturer engineering guide; HC-50 data in the current master dataset are from the 2020 manufacturer TDS.
2. The source tables do not state measurement uncertainty or detailed test methods.
3. The pressure basis for the thermophysical-property tables is not stated.
4. Grade concentration fractions and their mass/volume basis are not disclosed in the property tables.
5. These are manufacturer-reported values, not independent laboratory measurements.
6. No corrosion, compatibility, electrical, stability, or lifecycle testing is represented by this comparison.

## Next validation step

Test all five grades in one calibrated loop at matched inlet temperatures and heat loads. Report separate equal-mass-flow, equal-volumetric-flow, and equal-pump-power experiments, with at least three repeats per condition and propagated measurement uncertainty. Record the exact product lot and obtain manufacturer certificates or concentration verification where available. Only then should grade-specific thermohydraulic performance be ranked.
