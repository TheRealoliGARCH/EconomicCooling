# Controlled coolant comparison protocol

## Objective
Estimate comparative thermal and hydraulic performance of exact coolant formulations under matched service conditions. The protocol distinguishes intrinsic property differences from system-constraint effects; it does not presume that any two fluids are equivalent.

## Test article and loop
- Use a closed, temperature-controlled loop with interchangeable test sections of identical internal geometry, length, roughness, fittings and orientation.
- Record the full wetted-material bill of materials, seals, pump type, heat exchanger, filter and reservoir.
- Clean and dry the loop between fluids using a documented procedure; verify carryover and residual dilution.
- Use a degassed, well-mixed sample. Record product lot, age, storage history, formulation certificate and dilution-water quality.

## Instrumentation
- Calibrated mass-flow meter, or volumetric flow meter with simultaneous density measurement.
- Matched, calibrated inlet/outlet temperature sensors in well-mixed locations.
- Differential-pressure transducer across the defined test section and separate pressure sensors where needed.
- Electrical power analyser for pump input; record pump speed and operating point.
- Calibrated heat-input measurement and ambient/loop heat-loss characterisation.
- Fluid sampling for composition, pH, conductivity and degradation products before and after testing.

## Test matrix
Run three distinct comparison regimes; do not collapse them into one ranking:
1. Equal mass flow.
2. Equal volumetric flow.
3. Equal pump electrical power.

For each regime, use multiple flow setpoints spanning the intended Reynolds-number range. Repeat at matched inlet temperatures and heat loads. Include steady-state and transient runs where the application requires them. Keep the same wall heat flux or heat-source boundary condition within a comparison block.

## Measured outcomes
Calculate heat duty from calibrated calorimetry and enthalpy/property data where available. Report:
- heat duty and outlet temperature rise;
- pressure drop and hydraulic resistance;
- pump electrical power and estimated hydraulic power;
- heat-transfer coefficient with correlation and property-evaluation convention stated;
- Reynolds, Prandtl and Nusselt numbers;
- maximum wall and bulk temperatures;
- stability, cavitation margin and observed flow anomalies.

## Quality and uncertainty
- Calibrate sensors before and after the campaign.
- Conduct at least three repeat runs per condition, with randomized fluid order where practical.
- Propagate sensor and property uncertainty into heat duty, pressure drop, pump power and derived coefficients.
- Report confidence intervals or expanded uncertainty, calibration traceability, sampling frequency, filtering and steady-state criteria.
- Treat manufacturer typical values as comparison references, not substitutes for measured loop properties.

## Materials and lifecycle testing
Run separate coupon tests for corrosion and compatibility at relevant temperature, pressure and oxygen exposure. Assess fouling, particulate generation, seal swelling, electrical conductivity, fluid degradation, disposal requirements and replenishment. Short-duration heat-transfer tests cannot establish long-term chemical or materials equivalence.

## Reporting and equivalence decision
Publish raw time series, calibration certificates, data-cleaning code, exact product identifiers, lot numbers and test geometry. Define tolerances before analysis. An equivalence claim requires all pre-specified thermal and hydraulic outcomes to lie within declared margins, with uncertainty accounted for. Report equal-mass-flow, equal-volume-flow and equal-pump-power results separately. Thermohydraulic equivalence does not establish chemical, electrical, safety, corrosion, environmental or lifecycle equivalence.
